Do I Need a Brand Voice Guide?

If you've ever said "that doesn't sound like us," you already know the answer.

Most businesses don't think about brand voice until something goes wrong. A social post goes out that feels off. A contractor writes a bio that sounds nothing like the rest of the site. Someone new joins and immediately starts communicating in a completely different register. Suddenly the brand feels like three different people.

A brand voice guide fixes that. Here's what it actually is and whether you need one.

What a brand voice guide is

It's a document that defines how your business sounds in writing. Not what you sell, but how you talk about it. It covers your tone, your vocabulary, the things you say and the things you don't, and how that shifts depending on context. A caption is different from an email, which is different from a proposal, but they should all feel like they came from the same place.

What it isn't

It's not a logo. It's not a style guide for fonts and colors. And it's not a list of rules that makes everything sound robotic. A good brand voice guide actually makes writing easier, not harder, because decisions are already made.

Who needs one

If you're the only person writing for your business and you always will be, you might be able to skip it. Your voice is already consistent because it's just yours.

But if anyone else ever touches your content, whether that's a contractor, a new hire, an agency, or a tool like AI, a brand voice guide is how you make sure the output still sounds like you. Without it, you're editing everything from scratch every time.

What happens without one

Inconsistency. Not just in tone but in how people perceive your business. When your Instagram sounds casual, your emails sound corporate, and your website sounds like it was written by a committee, customers feel it even if they can't name it. Trust erodes when a brand doesn't feel like a coherent thing.

So do you need one?

If your business is growing, if you're starting to bring in help, or if you've ever looked at something published under your name and thought "that's not quite right," yes. You need one.

It doesn't have to be long. It has to be specific. The difference between "we're friendly" and "we write like we're talking to a smart friend who doesn't have time to waste" is the difference between a guide that helps and one that sits in a folder.
